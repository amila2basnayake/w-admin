# State water enforcement penalties — comparison

> **THE POINT OF THIS DOC:** penalty SCALE differs by an **order of magnitude** across states.
> NSW civil maxima now reach ~$10M; WA's individual ceiling under the 1914 Act is ~$10,000. Never
> quote one state's scale when advising on another. Penalty figures are fast-moving — verify
> current maxima before quoting.

## Per-state notes

### NSW
Highest-penalty jurisdiction since the Stronger Enforcement and Penalties reform (passed
13 Nov 2025, provisions from 1 Jan 2026): civil maxima ~$10M corporations / ~$5M individuals or 5x
water value, plus monetary benefit orders and daily penalties. Six-figure criminal fines were
already routine for serious matters. See `nsw-enforcement-penalties.md`.

### VIC
Water Act 1989 offences — unauthorised take/use and interfering with an authority's works — are
prosecuted by water corporations in the Magistrates' Court. Scale is modest for small-scale channel
theft: a Shepparton irrigator who switched a GMW outlet to local mode to divert unmetered water was
fined **$2,500** (2024). Conviction, licence consequences and publicity still follow; but
projecting NSW-scale six-figure fines onto a Victorian single-diversion fact pattern overstates.

### QLD
Water Act 2000 (Qld). Unauthorised take (s 808) covers more than pumping violations: taking water
**without a certified/validated meter is itself unauthorised take** — a Coastal Burnett irrigator
was fined **$16,690 by penalty infringement notice** (2025) for pumping on a lapsed meter
certification, no court needed. **False or misleading reporting is a separate serious charge**: a
western Queensland quarry operator under-reporting extraction of riverine quarry material by ~3x
was fined **$630,000** plus $66,358.90 royalties and costs (2023). Watering beyond the licensed
area is also an offence.

### WA
Rights in Water and Irrigation Act 1914 — **notably LOW maxima** (individual ceiling ~$10,000),
which WA has faced pressure to raise. But low penalty ≠ lawful: taking after a licence has
**expired is still unlawful take** — landownership confers no right to keep pumping while a
renewal is "sorted out" (Radich, Joondalup Magistrates Court 2022: $3,000 + $788.30 costs on two
charges).

### SA and TAS
No enforcement outcomes or penalty maxima were mined from verified sources for SA or TAS
(verify — under review). Both regulate take through licensing regimes with offence provisions;
do not quote figures for these states until sourced. Treat any SA/TAS penalty question as
requiring fresh verification.

## Advisory rules of thumb

- Strict liability is the norm for take/works offences in NSW and equivalent regimes — intent goes
  to penalty, not guilt.
- Regulators increasingly use administrative penalties (PINs) that issue without any court step.
- Costs orders can dwarf fines (NSW Harris: $60k fines, ~$450k costs).
