# Basic landholder / riparian rights — state by state

## Headline rule (all states)

In every Australian state these rights stop at **commercial use**. Irrigating a crop for sale,
watering stock raised for commercial sale beyond what the land normally carries, feedlots,
dairies and other intensive enterprises all require a licence/entitlement. And in most states
the **works** used to take the water (bore, pump, dam) are **separately licensed or approved**
even when the take itself is licence-free.

## NSW — basic landholder rights (Water Management Act 2000, Ch 3 Pt 1)

Three rights, no water access licence needed:

- **Domestic and stock rights (s 52)** — land with river, lake or estuary frontage, or overlying
  an aquifer: take for domestic consumption and stock watering. Covers non-intensive watering of
  stock ordinarily kept on the land; does NOT cover irrigation or intensive/commercial stock
  enterprises (e.g. feedlots).
- **Harvestable rights (s 53)** — capture a proportion of rainfall runoff in dams. Currently
  **10%** of average annual runoff in coastal-draining and central inland-draining catchments;
  the Western Division may capture all runoff. History: the coastal limit was lifted to **30% in
  May 2022**, then **returned to 10% in September 2023** pending sustainability assessment. A
  Coastal Harvestable Rights Review is ongoing — the coastal percentage is **(verify — under
  review)**. Sources published 2022–2023 saying 30% are stale. Dam capacity is calculated via
  the MHRDC online calculator; larger dams or dams on larger streams need licensing.
- **Native title rights** — as determined under the Native Title Act 1993 (Cth).

Works still need approval: a bore for domestic and stock use requires a **water supply work
approval** before construction/use ("It is an offence under the WMA to construct or use a water
supply work without an approval" — WaterNSW), built by a licensed driller. A BLR-only bore
approval has effect until cancelled.

## VIC — s 8 private right (Water Act 1989)

- Applies where the waterway **abuts or traverses** the property and **no Crown land, road
  reserve or public purpose reserve separates** the property from the water.
- Free and licence-free for **domestic and stock only**: household purposes, watering stock and
  pets, and irrigating a **kitchen garden up to 0.1 ha**.
- **Excludes** dairies, piggeries, feedlots, poultry and other intensive or commercial uses.
- A licence may still be needed to construct and operate the **works** used to access the water.

## QLD — s 96 riparian right + overland flow (Water Act 2000)

- **s 96**: an owner of land **adjoining** a watercourse, lake or spring may take water for
  stock or domestic purposes without a water entitlement. "Stock purposes" means watering stock
  of a number that would **normally be depastured** on the land; "land" extends to contiguous
  land in the same ownership. Stock/domestic take on land NOT adjoining the watercourse needs a
  water licence, and the domestic right can be excluded by regulation for declared land.
- **Overland flow**: default position is take for **any purpose** unless a moratorium notice or
  water plan limits it. In regulated plan areas a licence may be needed and new works are
  assessable/accepted development under the Planning Act 2016; maintenance must not increase
  storage capacity.
- **No-authorisation-at-all cases**: firefighting and equipment testing, camping and watering
  travelling stock (no pumps/constructed facilities), taking by hand for personal use, and
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander traditional/cultural purposes — all limitable during
  declared shortages.

## SA — s 100(5) stock and domestic (Landscape South Australia Act 2019)

- No licence or authorisation needed to take and use water for **stock and domestic** purposes,
  continuing even in most prescribed areas.
- **Exceptions** — a licence may be required in some areas, e.g. the **River Murray Prescribed
  Watercourse**, the **Northern Adelaide Plains**, and **dams > 5 ML** capacity in the Western
  Mount Lofty Ranges Prescribed Water Resources Area.
- Nobody owns water in SA — the Act confers rights to access, take and use it.

## WA — riparian/stock-and-domestic exemptions (RiWI Act 1914)

- Unauthorised take is prohibited except under another right (e.g. a riparian right); exemptions
  exist for certain **stock and domestic** uses.
- The exemptions **never cover commercial irrigation**: surface water in a proclaimed area, and
  **artesian groundwater anywhere in the state**, require a 5C licence.
- Interfering with the bed and banks of a watercourse needs separate permits (ss 11, 17, 21A).

## TAS — Part 5 rights (Water Management Act 1999): DOMESTIC ONLY

- Waterfront landholders may take water for **domestic use** without a licence.
- NRE Tas is explicit: riparian rights "are solely for domestic purposes and do not allow you to
  irrigate a paddock of any size if the purpose is to graze animals or grow crops which will
  later be sold." Watering stock for commercial sale, or irrigating commercial crops, requires a
  water licence. Tasmania's right is the narrowest of the six states.
