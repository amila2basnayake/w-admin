# Interstate trade mechanics — tagging, transfer, exchange rates, unbundling

## Two mechanisms for permanent (entitlement) interstate trade

| Mechanism | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Tagged trade** | The entitlement **stays registered in the source system/state**; only the place of use moves, under the MDB Agreement Schedule D and its procedures. (The *Tagging Entitlements for Extraction in another State Protocol 2010* was **repealed from 1 July 2026**; tagged trade is now handled under Schedule D and Basin Officials Committee procedures.) Victorian Water Register example: "when a water user located in the Murray system purchases a Goulburn water share, the water share source remains in the Goulburn, but it is tagged for use in the Murray (this means they still receive Goulburn allocations)". The QLD Border Rivers statement of the same concept: the water "will remain registered or issued in, and subject to the terms and conditions and management rules of, the state of origin". |
| **Transfer (change of location/ownership)** | A permanent trade recorded as a change of ownership and/or location on the register. In Victoria a transfer of a water share "does not transfer the allocation account (ABA) or any of the allocation held in the ABA". |

Interstate trades require the **approval of the water authorities in both states**, following a
protocol agreed between governments under the **Murray-Darling Basin Agreement** (Schedule D).

### 2026 Schedule D restructure

From **1 July 2026** six 2010 Schedule D protocols — including *Permissible Transfers between Trading
Zones*, *Tagging Entitlements for Extraction in another State*, *Processing Interstate Transfers of
Water Allocations*, *Processing Interstate Exchange Rate Transfers*, *Conversion Factors and Exchange
Rates*, and *Calculating Cap Adjustments* — were **repealed** and replaced by the **Processing
Interstate and Intervalley Trade Protocol 2026** (F2026L00861) and the **Adjusting Valley Accounts
and State Transfer Accounts (No. 2) Protocol 2026** (F2026L00905). Trading-zone definitions and
per-zone conditions now sit in **procedures** made by the Basin Officials Committee. Full text in
library: `mdba-schedule-d-processing-interstate-intervalley-trade-protocol-2026`,
`mdba-schedule-d-adjusting-valley-accounts-state-transfer-no2-protocol-2026`.

### Tagged-use restrictions (current)

- **Grandfathered tags are still relevant — only the *exemption* was removed.** On **1 July 2024**
  the Basin Plan **s 12.23(2)** *exemption*, which let 'grandfathered' tagged entitlements avoid
  allocation trade (inter-valley) restrictions, was removed (Water Amendment (Restoring Our Rivers)
  Act 2023, on the ACCC's 2021 recommendation). Take under those tagged entitlements is now
  **subject to the same allocation trade restrictions** (IVT limits, Barmah Choke balance) as all
  other tagged arrangements. The **grandfathered tagged accounts themselves still exist and remain
  recognised** — MDBA Barmah Choke balance material reports a volume (reported as ~15 GL) reserved
  for grandfathered tagged accounts, set aside from the general opening trade opportunity (verify
  the current figure against the MDBA Choke opening-balance page).
- Victoria has applied **long-term restrictions on tagged water use** against the Goulburn-to-
  Murray trade limit since **30 November 2021**. The last transitional carve-out (Lower Broken
  Creek) expired **30 June 2026**.

## Temporary (allocation) interstate trade

- Allocation trade transfers a volume of unused water allocation from a seller's account to a
  buyer's; permitted only when the unused allocation in the seller's account is at least the trade
  volume. Applications go to the seller's water corporation/authority; trade is permitted between
  trading zones within a state and **interstate**, subject to IVT limits and the Barmah Narrows
  rule (see `cross-ivt-open-close-mechanics`, `cross-barmah-choke`).
- Interstate allocation trade in the southern connected Basin is **1:1 (megalitre for
  megalitre)**: the declared exchange rate into SA is conservatively set at **1.0** (any extra
  security accrues to the river), and there is **no per-trade conveyance-loss deduction** —
  transmission losses are handled in river operations under the MDB Agreement, not charged to the
  trade (MDBA exchange-rate declarations).
- **Bulk water trade adjustments** between NSW and Victorian storage accounts have been trialled
  since July 2019 to reduce the operational cost of interstate trade.
- Basin Plan overlay: trade of **carried-over** allocation cannot be restricted (Basin Plan
  s 12.11) and exchange rates are prohibited in regulated systems (s 12.21, narrow s 12.22
  exceptions) — the 1:1 interstate declarations sit within those exceptions. See
  `cth-basin-plan-2012-water-trading-rules`.

## Exchange rates (historical, intra-state)

Before unbundling, permanent inter-system trade within Victoria used an **exchange-rate system**:
"an entitlement trade from the Goulburn to the Murray resulted in cancellation of Goulburn
entitlement and issue of a new Murray entitlement, the volume of which was determined by the
relevant exchange rate" — a conversion factor reconciling different reliabilities between systems.
Tagged trade replaced this model for interstate entitlement trade, and Basin Plan **s 12.21** now
prohibits exchange rates in regulated systems (with narrow s 12.22 exceptions).

## Unbundling

- **Unbundling** separated the old land-tied water right into independently tradable components —
  in Victoria: **water share** (high/low reliability) + **delivery share** + **water-use
  licence/registration**.
- Victorian dates (verified): northern Victorian regulated systems became declared systems and
  unbundled **1 July 2007** (seven declared surface-water systems); southern Victoria (Werribee,
  Bacchus Marsh, Macalister/Thomson) **1 July 2008**.
- Purpose: "greater flexibility to irrigators and diverters to manage their water use and their
  risks, through easier water trade and increased entitlement certainty".
- NSW achieved a comparable separation via the Water Management Act 2000's licence framework
  (share/extraction components separate from land) and SA via water access entitlements under its
  WAP framework — see the state docs; exact NSW/SA unbundling timelines were not verified here.
