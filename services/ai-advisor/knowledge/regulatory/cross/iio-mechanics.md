# IIO mechanics — water rights inside irrigation districts

Applies to customers of irrigation infrastructure operators such as **Murray Irrigation (MIL)**,
**Murrumbidgee Irrigation (MI)** and **Coleambally Irrigation (CICL)** in NSW, and to
**Goulburn-Murray Water** irrigation districts in Victoria.

## The structural point

- The **operator holds the bulk water access licence** with the state. Customers do not appear
  on the state register for that water.
- Customers hold **internal entitlements** (shares in the operator's bulk licence) plus
  **delivery entitlements / delivery shares** (a share of channel capacity).
- Consequence: an IIO customer asking "where do I lodge my trade?" or "what's my water balance?"
  gets an **operator answer** — the operator's own portal and transfer rules — not a
  WaterNSW/iWAS (or state register) answer. Murray Irrigation customers, for example, check the
  Water Allocation Report in the MIL Customer Portal.
- **Transformation is the exit path**: a customer who wants a state-registered entitlement in
  their own name transforms their internal entitlement out of the operator's bulk licence (a
  right protected under the Basin water trading rules). Until then, everything runs through the
  operator.

## Trades and carryover run on operator rules

- Trades of internal entitlements and allocations are processed under the **operator's transfer
  rules**; entitlements and delivery entitlements generally transfer **only within the service
  area**, and delivery entitlement trades are subject to operator approval of channel capacity
  (e.g. MI approves DE trades against spare channel capacity).
- Carryover is an **operator product with its own caps** — these are operator rules that change
  seasonally, so **verify with the operator** before advising:
  - **MIL**: carryover up to **50% of Class C entitlement**; excess forfeited; carryover counts
    toward next season's allocation, which is capped at 110% of entitlements (operator rule —
    verify current season).
  - **CICL**: carryover up to **30% of general security**; not available on High Security or G
    class (operator rule — verify current season).
- Price reporting and 5-year record-keeping obligations under the Water Amendment (Restoring
  Our Rivers) Act 2023 reach into IIO trades too (per CICL guidance).

## Delivery entitlements / delivery shares are separate from water

- A delivery entitlement (NSW IIOs) or delivery share (Vic districts) is a right to have water
  **delivered** — a share of channel/pipeline capacity, typically expressed as a flow rate. It
  is a distinct product from the water entitlement: owning water without delivery rights (or
  vice versa) is possible and is a classic due-diligence trap.
- In Victoria the delivery share **stays with the land** and transfers automatically on sale;
  the water share does not.
- Delivery rights carry **termination fees**: in Victoria, terminating or reducing a delivery
  share costs **10 times the annual infrastructure access fee** (Victorian Water Register,
  delivery shares page — "can be significant"). NSW IIOs impose termination charges under their
  own pricing; check the operator's schedule.
- Membership/holding minimums can apply (e.g. CICL membership requires 10 delivery entitlements,
  10 shares and minimum 10 ML/yr use or trade — operator rule, verify).

## Practical triage for the advisor

- First question for any trade/carryover/balance query: **is the customer inside an IIO
  district?** If yes, route to the operator's rules and portal.
- State-register answers (WaterNSW, Victorian Water Register, iWAS) apply to the operator's
  bulk licence and to transformed entitlements — not to internal holdings.
- Operator-specific numbers (carryover caps, allocation caps, DE minimums) change seasonally:
  always verify against the operator's current-season announcements.
