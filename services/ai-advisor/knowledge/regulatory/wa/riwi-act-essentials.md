# WA — Rights in Water and Irrigation Act 1914 essentials

Administered by the **Department of Water and Environmental Regulation (DWER)**. WA is not a
Murray-Darling Basin state and its framework diverges sharply from the eastern states.

## When a licence is required

- **Artesian groundwater**: licence required **everywhere in WA**.
- **Proclaimed areas**: licence required for non-artesian/unconfined groundwater and surface
  water, unless a valid exemption applies (limited stock and domestic exemptions exist — never
  for commercial irrigation).
- Interfering with or obstructing the bed and banks of a watercourse needs separate permits
  under ss 11, 17 or 21A.

## The 5C licence (licence to take water)

- Authorises taking a specified maximum annual volume (the **licensed entitlement**) from a
  named resource, with terms, conditions, and an issue and expiry date.
- **Fixed term**: DWER policy sets a **10-year standard tenure**; shorter terms may be granted
  in certain circumstances. This contrasts with VIC water shares and SA water access
  entitlements, which are ongoing.
- **Renewal is not automatic**: renewal applications must be lodged before expiry; DWER warns an
  expired licence may not be reinstatable and recommends applying **60–90 days before expiry**.

## The 26D licence (construct or alter a well)

- Required to construct or alter a well/bore.
- A 26D licence **does not authorise the take** of groundwater — you need the 5C licence as
  well. Two separate instruments for one bore-and-pump operation.

## Transfers, trades and agreements — same resource area only

Three mechanisms move a 5C entitlement between people:

- **Transfer** — permanent change of licensee, same location.
- **Trade** — permanent movement of an entitlement to another person taking from another
  location (typically in fully allocated areas).
- **Agreement (Sch 1 cl 30)** — "a form of lease" temporarily assigning the entitlement.

All three can occur **only within the same water resource area** (same groundwater subarea and
aquifer, or same surface water area). WA water cannot be traded to a different aquifer,
catchment or state.

## No MDB-style unbundling

- The 5C licence bundles the take entitlement, conditions and location in one fixed-term
  instrument. There are no separately tradeable perpetual water shares, delivery shares or
  water-use licences as in Victoria.
- A broker pitching WA water as a standalone open-market asset is overstating it: entitlements
  move only by transfer/trade/cl 30 agreement inside the resource area, and the underlying
  licence expires and must be renewed.

## Compliance

- Penalties under the RiWI Act sit on a low scale by eastern-states standards (no dollar
  figures in the source set — verify current penalty provisions before advising). Contrast NSW's
  post-November-2025 civil penalty regime.

## Practical answers

- "How long does my WA licence last?" → ~10 years standard; diarise renewal 60–90 days early.
- "Can I sell my entitlement to a grower in another catchment?" → No; same water resource area
  only.
- "Does my 26D bore licence let me pump?" → No; take requires the 5C licence.
