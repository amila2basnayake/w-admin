# NSW water enforcement and penalties

> **FAST-MOVING.** The penalty landscape changed fundamentally in November 2025 and provisions are
> still commencing. Any figure below may be superseded — verify against current NRAR / NSW
> Government material before quoting a maximum. Sources published before late 2025 give much lower
> numbers and are stale.

## Who enforces

- The **Natural Resources Access Regulator (NRAR)** is the NSW water compliance and enforcement
  agency. It investigates, issues penalty notices and directions, and prosecutes in the Land and
  Environment Court (LEC) and Local Court.
- **WaterNSW** historically ran prosecutions before NRAR took over the function — the Harris and
  Barlow matters below were WaterNSW prosecutions. Older case names reflect this.

## The November 2025 reform — Stronger Enforcement and Penalties

The **Water Management Legislation Amendment (Stronger Enforcement and Penalties) Bill 2025**
**passed NSW Parliament on 13 November 2025**, with provisions **commencing from 1 January 2026**.
It created a natural resources **civil penalty regime** in the Land and Environment Court alongside
the existing criminal offences. (Commentary: KWM "Water Regulation Series (part 1): New civil
penalties for water offences in NSW"; Gilbert + Tobin "NSW Water Management reforms: stronger
enforcement and penalties"; Ashurst "A new age of enforcement under the NSW Water Management Act".)

Key elements:

- **Civil penalty maxima** for the most serious breaches (e.g. unauthorised take): approximately
  **$10 million for corporations** (≈ $9,999,000) and **$5 million for individuals**, **or 5x the
  value of the water taken**, plus **daily penalties** for continuing contraventions.
- **Monetary benefit orders** — stripping the financial gain from the breach.
- **Cost recovery** on compliance directions.
- **Expanded investigative powers**, including drone-assisted entry.
- **Expanded licence suspension and cancellation grounds.**

Do not conflate this with the earlier (2024) penalty-notice increase — that lifted on-the-spot PINs
for corporations to $15,400 (verify — under review) and is distinct from the civil regime.

## Prior criminal tiers (for contrast — pre-reform scale)

- Before the 2025 reform, the criminal maxima for the most serious offences were roughly
  **$2 million (corporations) / $500,000 (individuals)** — an order of magnitude below the new
  civil maxima.
- Per-offence maxima seen in the decided cases: ~$247,500 (Harris-era s 91G breach), $247,000 and
  $505,000 (O'Haire charges). Criminal prosecution remains available alongside the civil track.

## Penalty infringement notices

NRAR can issue on-the-spot penalty notices without court proceedings for lower-tier breaches;
corporate PIN amounts were increased in 2024 (to $15,400 — verify, under review). Escalation to
LEC prosecution or civil penalty proceedings remains available.

## Decided matters (history)

All pre-date the 2025 civil regime — they show the *criminal* sentencing scale, now the floor, not
the ceiling.

- **Harris** — *WaterNSW v Harris (No 3)* [2020] NSWLEC 18; sentence [2023] NSWLEC 33: couple
  convicted of taking 1,896 ML from the Barwon-Darling below a flow-condition threshold; fined
  $40,000 + $20,000 but ordered to pay ~$450,000 prosecutor's costs — costs dwarfed the fines.
- **Barlow** — *WaterNSW v Barlow* [2019] NSWLEC 30: strict-liability convictions for pumping
  during an embargo and with a non-working meter; ~$190,000 total fines. First post-Matthews-inquiry
  Barwon-Darling prosecution.
- **Budvalt** — *NRAR v Budvalt Pty Ltd; NRAR v Harris* [2020] NSWLEC 113: 2 km unapproved channel;
  no unlawful take proven, convicted anyway (s 91B works offence is strict liability); $252,000
  fine plus newspaper publication orders.
- **O'Haire** — *NRAR v O'Haire* [2020] NSWLEC (Pepper J): eight charges of unlawful take
  (~1,378 ML, Murray at Euston), deliberate and repeat conduct; $131,250 total after guilty plea.
- **Jindalee** — *Jindalee Road Wines Pty Ltd v NRAR; Littore v NRAR* [2025] NSWCCA 215: buried
  meter-bypass pipework taking ~365 ML from an irrigation pipeline; NRAR's largest result;
  CCA-resentenced aggregate fines of $558,000 ($345,000 company / $213,000 manager personally).

## Advisory notes

- Most NSW take/works offences are **strict liability** — intent and ignorance go to penalty, not
  guilt. "I held a licence" or "the meter was faulty" is not a defence.
- Contractors can be independently liable for unapproved works.
- Never quote the NSW penalty scale for another state — see the cross-jurisdiction summary; scales
  differ by an order of magnitude.
