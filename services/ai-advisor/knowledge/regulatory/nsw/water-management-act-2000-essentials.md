# NSW — Water Management Act 2000 essentials

The Water Management Act 2000 (NSW) ("WMA 2000") is the framework for water sharing, licensing and
trading in NSW.

## Objects and principles

- **s 3** (object, quoted in the fetched review report): "to provide for the sustainable and
  integrated management of the water sources of the State for the benefit of both present and
  future generations", applying ecologically-sustainable-development principles, protecting water
  sources and ecosystems, and providing for orderly, efficient and equitable sharing.
- **s 5** sets the water management principles: general principles s 5(2); **water sharing
  principles s 5(3)** (sharing must protect the water source, dependent ecosystems and basic
  landholder rights); plus use/drainage/floodplain/controlled-activity/aquifer principles
  (s 5(4)-(8)).
- **s 9(1)**: decision-makers must act in accordance with the principles and, for water sharing,
  **give priority to the s 5(3) principles in the order they are set out** — the priority order is
  statutory.

## Water access licences (WALs)

- **s 56**: a WAL has two components — a **share component** (a share of the water available to its
  category each year) and an **extraction component** (rights over flows, times, rates of take).
  The extraction component can be dealt with separately through the s 71 dealings (amendment under
  s 71S).
- Licence **categories** are established by **s 57** (high security, general security, supplementary
  water, conveyance, local water utility, domestic and stock, major utility) — see
  `nsw-access-licence-categories`.
- Water is credited to licence accounts via **available water determinations (s 59)** — see
  `nsw-available-water-determinations`.

## Planning framework

- **Water sharing plans (WSPs)** are statutory plans made under the Act, one per water source, with
  a 10-year term; **s 20** sets the core provisions every WSP must contain and **s 21** the
  additional provisions a WSP may contain — including water-account operation, **carryover of
  credits, and the maximum credit in an account** (the statutory hook for carryover/account-limit
  rules). Plans can be **extended past their 10-year term** (s 43A, on Natural Resources Commission
  recommendation) — the six 2016 inland regulated-river WSPs were extended two years to
  30 June 2028. See `nsw-water-sharing-plans`.

## Trading

Licence and allocation trades are "dealings" under the **s 71** series (permanent share assignment
s 71Q, temporary allocation assignment s 71T, whole-licence transfer s 71M, tagging via s 71W,
etc.) — see `nsw-dealings-s71`.

## WAL Register reform (2024-2026)

The **Water Management Amendment (Water Access Licence Register Reform) Act 2024** (No 69)
amends the Act's WAL Register provisions. First tranche commenced **31 Oct 2025**: a positive
obligation on the Minister to keep the WAL Register up to date, and a requirement for a publicly
accessible, searchable NSW water register. Remaining provisions roll out through 2026 — verify
current commencement status before advising on register/transparency obligations.
