# TAS — Water Management Act 1999 essentials

Administered by **NRE Tas** (Department of Natural Resources and Environment Tasmania).

## When a licence is required

- A water licence is required before water can be taken from a river or stream, or stored in a
  farm dam, for **commercial purposes**. NRE Tas: "You must have a water licence to take, trap
  or store water for the use of watering stock for commercial sale or for crop irrigation."
- The licence carries one or more **water allocations**, obtained either by establishing a right
  to take water at the licence location or by transferring an allocation from another licence.
  Conditions can attach to both the licence and the allocation.

## Surety levels (allocation reliability)

Allocations are ranked by **surety level** — how reliably the water is expected to be
available. When restrictions bite, lower-surety allocations are restricted before higher ones.

- **Surety 1** — highest priority, notionally better than **95%** reliability; covers domestic
  use, stock and town water supply.
- **Surety 5** — notionally about **80%** reliability (eight years in ten); covers direct
  extraction and winter storage in dams for irrigation and other commercial purposes.
- **Surety 6** — notionally available at **less than ~80%** reliability.
- The source suite recorded the scheme as having **eight** tiers in total **(verify — under
  review)**; confirm the full ladder against NRE Tas's surety-levels page before citing a count.

This is Tasmania's analogue to Victoria's high/low-reliability shares or NSW's high
security/general security, but with a multi-tier ladder rather than two classes.

## Part 5 rights — domestic ONLY

- Waterfront landholders may take water for **domestic use** without a licence.
- The right expressly does **not** allow irrigating **any paddock of any size** where the
  purpose is grazing animals or growing crops for later sale, nor watering stock for commercial
  sale. Commercial take of any kind needs the licence above.

## Dams — a separate approval

- All dams (including levees and weirs) are regulated under the Act and the Safety of Dams
  regulations: a **Permit to Undertake Dam Works** is needed to construct, remove or modify a
  dam.
- The dam works permit covers the **structure only**. A water licence may also be required if
  you collect water into the dam directly from a stream or pump water into it from another
  source — the permit is not a right to take or store water for commercial use.

## Transfers — NRE Tas approval required

- A water licence or water allocation can be transferred **temporarily (leased)** or
  **permanently (sold)**, but only with NRE Tas approval.
- The recipient of an allocation transfer must **also hold a water licence, or submit a licence
  application at the same time** — a neighbour without a licence cannot simply take your water.
- **Sole exception**: NRE Tas may, at its discretion, grant a temporary transfer of **not more
  than 21 days** without the recipient holding a licence, where a **dire water shortage
  affecting livelihood** can be demonstrated.

## Practical answers

- "Can I pump from the creek for a market crop?" → Licence required; Part 5 does not cover it.
- "Is my dam permit enough?" → No; the permit covers the works, the licence covers the take.
- "Can I lease water to an unlicensed neighbour?" → Only the ≤21-day dire-shortage exception.
