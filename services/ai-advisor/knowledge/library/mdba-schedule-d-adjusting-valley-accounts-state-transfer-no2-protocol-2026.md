# Murray-Darling Basin Agreement (Schedule D – Adjusting Valley Accounts and State Transfer Accounts) (No. 2) Protocol 2026

Source: uploaded file `MDBA (Schedule D _ Adjusting Valley Accounts and State Transfer).pdf` (Commonwealth legislative instrument (protocol), dated 2026-06-30). Added to the knowledge base 2026-08-24.

## Key points

- The instrument is the Murray-Darling Basin Agreement (Schedule D – Adjusting Valley Accounts and State Transfer Accounts) (No. 2) Protocol 2026, made under clause 6 of Schedule D to the Murray-Darling Basin Agreement and section 18E of the Water Act 2007, dated 30 June 2026 and commencing 1 July 2026.
- It applies in respect to interstate transfers and intervalley transfers (clause 5).
- 'back trade' means an interstate or intervalley transfer from one trading zone to another that is no greater in volume than the net volume of preceding transfers between the same zones in the opposite direction and the volume available in the relevant valley account (clause 6).
- 'volumetric trigger' means when volumes on the NSW and Victorian sides of Hume Reservoir in a given month indicate more than a 50% chance the reservoir will spill (internally and/or physically) before the end of the next inflow season, based on Authority estimates (clause 6).
- State Contracting Governments must reduce a valley account balance by the volume of water spilled, with the reduction less than the lesser of the volume spilled from designated water supply sources in that valley or the balance carried over from a previous financial year, minus prior deliveries before spill (clause 9).
- Water released in advance of an anticipated spill may be counted as spilled, but water added in the current financial year must not be counted as spilled (clause 9).
- If a direction does not specify a designated water supply source or site, NSW must supply water for Murrumbidgee, Yanco or Billabong valley accounts to the River Murray via the Snowy Mountains Scheme (clause 11(3)(a)).
- A direction under subclause 9(3) of Schedule D can result in a valley account being overdrawn if the relevant State consents (note to clause 11).
- A designated water supply source is one prescribed as a designated reservoir under the 2010 Protocol before its repeal, or one the Committee decides is robust and measurable meeting end-of-system flow, third-party/environmental impact and accounting-complexity tests (clause 12).
- When adjusting deliveries between NSW and Victoria, the Authority must adjust the volume deliverable from Dartmouth Reservoir if the volumetric trigger is met, otherwise from Hume Reservoir (clause 13(4)).
- The Authority may require a State to appoint an independent auditor for a valley account where it believes there is an unresolved error or omission, and may require the account be altered and State transfer accounts recalculated (clause 16).
- The Authority must maintain and adjust the Barmah Choke entitlement record, including entries for transfers of entitlements from below to above the Barmah Choke recorded under the 2010 Permissible Transfers Protocol (clause 17).
- The Barmah Choke allocation account must be set at zero on 1 July each financial year, increased by water used under recorded entitlements and by transfers from below to above the Choke, and decreased by proposed transfers traded down from above to below the Choke (clause 18(5)).
- Environmental water entitlements from water savings below the Barmah Choke before 1 July 2007 under the Snowy Water Enquiry Deed must not be used to offset back trade from above to below the Choke (clause 18(2)).
- The Authority must maintain and publish on its website an automated Barmah Choke balance tool showing available transfer capacity across the Choke, and an approval authority may only record volumes against it if it proposes to approve the transfer (clause 19).
- Schedule 1 repeals the 2010 Protocol and the earlier 2026 Protocol; an Annexure lists 24 agreed principles of the Contracting Governments.

8  Maintaining accounts
 (1)  In maintaining the accounts for which they are responsible, the Authority and
each State Contracting Government must record and reconcile information
relating to interstate transfers and intervalley transfers as appropriate for each
account for the purpose of this protocol.
 (2)  State Contracting Governments must take reasonable steps to ensure the quality
and accuracy of information regarding interstate transfers and intervalley
transfers as part of maintaining valley accounts.
9  Accounting for water spilled
 (1)  The relevant State Contracting Government must reduce the balance of a valley
account by the volume of water spilled from the valley account, except as
otherwise provided for in this section.
 (2)  When a volume of water is released in advance of an anticipated spill, the
volume released may be included as part of the volume spilled for the purposes
of subsection (1).
 (3)  Water added to the account in the current financial year must not be included as
part of the volume spilled for the purposes of subsection (1).
 (4)  The reduction of the balance of a valley account must be less than the lesser of:
 (a)  the volume spilled from designated water supply sources in that valley; or
 (b)  the balance of the account carried over from a previous financial year;
   minus any deliveries made against the valley account for that valley prior to the
time of spill.

and implementing a tributary operating plan.

12  Designated water supply sources
 (1)  A designated water supply source is a water supply source for a relevant valley
account that:
 (a)  was prescribed as a designated reservoir for the relevant valley account by
the Murray-Darling Basin Agreement (Schedule D — Adjusting Valley
Accounts and State Transfer Accounts) Protocol 2010 immediately prior to
its repeal; or
 (b)  the Committee has decided is robust and measurable, provided the
Committee is satisfied that designating the water supply source would:
 (i)  ensure changes in end of system flows in the valley of origin are
acceptable having regard to changes in diversions due to trade; and
 (ii)  avoid unacceptable impacts on third party entitlements or the
environment, in either the valley or origin or valley of destination,
compared to if trade credited to the relevant valley account had not
occurred; and
 (iii)  balance accounting complexity with the materiality of the delivery
and impacts in the valley of origin and destination.
 (2)  A decision by the Committee under paragraph (1)(b) may include any conditions
or limitations on the use of the water supply source, including publication
requirements that must be satisfied before the water supply source can be used.
13  Adjusting deliveries of water
 (1)  This section applies when the Authority adjusts the delivery of State water
entitlements within the scope of clause 8 of Schedule D to the Agreement.
 (2)  The Authority must record adjustments to the delivery of State water
entitlements in the State transfer account for each affected State.
 (3)  The Authority may adjust the delivery of State water entitlements to more
accurately match the delivery of State water entitlements with expected demands
after consulting with the affected State Contracting Governments.
 (4)  When the Authority adjusts the delivery of State water entitlements between
New South Wales and Victoria, the Authority must adjust the volume of water
that may be delivered to either State from:
 (a)  Dartmouth Reservoir if the volumetric trigger is met at the time the
adjustment is made; or
 (b)  otherwise, Hume Reservoir.

17  Barmah Choke entitlement record
   As part of maintaining and adjusting the Barmah Choke entitlement record, the
Authority must:
 (a)  undertake relevant calculations and accounting, as required; and
 (b)  include in the record any entries relating to transfers of water access
entitlements from below the Barmah Choke in a State to above the Barmah
Choke in any State that were recorded under paragraph 8(2)(a) of the
Murray-Darling Basin Agreement (Schedule D — Permissible Transfers
between Trading Zones) Protocol 2010.
18  Accounting for the Barmah Choke allocation account
 (1)  For the Barmah Choke allocation account, the Authority must calculate and
account for:
 (a)  the opening balance by reference to the Barmah Choke entitlement record;
and
 (b)  interstate transfers and intervalley transfers relating to the account.
 (2)  Environmental water access entitlements derived from water savings made below
the Barmah Choke before 1 July 2007 for the purposes of the Snowy Water
Enquiry Deed must be accounted for in the following manner:

flows in the Snowy River must not, for the purposes of this protocol, be
used to offset back trade from above to below the Barmah Choke; and
 (b)  the volume of any such water access entitlement to be used to improve
flows in the River Murray must be counted as existing water access
entitlement below the Barmah Choke, and must not, for the purposes of
this protocol, be used to offset back trade from above to below the
Barmah Choke.
 (3)  Except for:
 (a)  water access entitlements that were transferred before 1 July 2007
(including the transfers referred to in paragraph (2)(a)); and
 (b)  environmental water access entitlements that were derived from water
savings made below the Barmah Choke after 1 July 2007 pursuant to the
Snowy Water Enquiry Deed to be used to improve flows in the

valley account that the State Contracting Government is responsible for maintaining,
including up-to-date information regarding the volume of water available under a valley
account.
13 State Contracting Governments will make certain valley account information generally
available in accordance with arrangements specified in procedures.

14 The Contracting Governments affirm that the Authority may direct that water standing to
the credit of a valley account for any valley be used for any purpose to which the Authority
may have regard under subclause 98(3) or 98(4) of the Agreement:
(a) subject to any tributary operating limits or rules made under applicable laws

(b) in consultation with the relevant State Contracting Government; and

(d) with regard to any applicable tributary operating plan.
15 State Contracting Governments will implement a direction made under subclause 9(3) of
Schedule D to the Agreement by supplying the water from a designated water supply source
to a designated site in accordance with the protocols and procedures.
16 A State Contracting Government is responsible for developing any proposal to use a new or
amended designated water supply source for the consideration of the Basin Official’s
Committee. A State Contracting Government will seek the agreement of the Authority
before presenting a proposal to the Basin Officials Committee. A proposal will set out the
circumstances in which the designated water supply source may be used, any conditions
attaching to its use, and how it complies with any existing aspects of the procedures that
apply.
17 In considering whether to make procedures specifying a new designated water supply
source and any conditions attaching to its use, the Basin Officials Committee will consider
whether designating the water supply source would:
(a) ensure changes in end of system flows in the valley of origin are the result of
acceptable having regard to changes in diversions due to trade; and
(b) would avoid unacceptable impacts on third party entitlements or the
environment, in either the valley or origin or valley of destination, compared
to if trade had not occurred; and
(c) would balance accounting complexity with the materiality of the delivery and
