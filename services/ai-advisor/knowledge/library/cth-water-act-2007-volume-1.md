# Water Act 2007 (Cth) — Compilation No. 37 (Volume 1, Parts 1-10A, ss 1-239W)

Source: uploaded file `Water Act 2007 volume 1.pdf` (Commonwealth statute (compilation), dated 2026-07-01). Added to the knowledge base 2026-08-24.

## Key points

- This is Water Act 2007 (No. 137, 2007) Compilation No. 37, compilation date 1 July 2026, showing the law as in force on that date; Volume 1 covers Parts 1 to 10A, sections 1 to 239W (Authorised Version C2026C00302, registered 17/07/2026).
- Includes amendments up to Act No. 111, 2023 and F2026L00815; uncommenced amendments are not shown in the text.
- Section 4 defines key terms: 'water access entitlement' means a perpetual or ongoing entitlement under State law to exclusive access to a share of the water resources of a water resource plan area; 'water allocation' means the specific volume allocated to water access entitlements in a given water accounting period.
- Section 4 defines 'water access right' to include stock and domestic rights, riparian rights, a water access entitlement and a water allocation; 'tradeable water rights' means water access rights, water delivery rights or irrigation rights.
- Section 4 defines 'consumptive use' as the use of water for private benefit consumptive purposes including irrigation, industry, urban and stock and domestic use; and 'eligible tradeable water right' is given detailed meaning in section 6A.
- Section 6A defines 'eligible tradeable water right' and provides (subsection (2)) that for Parts 5 and 5A it does not include a right that is a financial product within section 761A of the Corporations Act 2001.
- Part 2 establishes the Basin Plan machinery: the Authority prepares the Plan for Ministerial adoption (ss 41-44), sets long-term average sustainable diversion limits (s 23), and the Authority must review the Basin Plan at least every 10 years (s 50).
- Part 4 provides for Basin water charge rules (Division 1) and water market rules (Division 2), with the ACCC monitoring water charges/compliance (s 94) and transformation arrangements/compliance (s 99).
- Part 5 establishes the Water Markets Intermediaries Code and a statutory trust accounting framework for eligible water markets intermediaries, including trust-account obligations (ss 100R-100Z) and ACCC enforcement powers.
- Part 5A ('Water markets decisions, insider trading and market manipulation') creates prohibitions on insider trading (Division 4, ss 101H-101JF) and market manipulation / false trading and market rigging (Division 4A, ss 101JG-101JK), with exceptions and civil penalties.
- 'Material effect' for Part 5A means a decision or information reasonably likely to influence persons who commonly acquire eligible tradeable water rights in deciding whether to acquire or dispose of such rights.
- Basin States are defined as New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and the Australian Capital Territory (s 4); ACCC means the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission.
- Enforcement (Part 8) provides for injunctions, declarations, civil penalties, infringement notices, enforceable undertakings and executive-officer liability; Part 10AC gives the ACCC powers to require information, documents and evidence (ss 239AJ-239AN).
- Objects of the Act (s 3) include managing Basin water resources in the national interest, returning overallocated resources to environmentally sustainable extraction, and ensuring water-market governance promotes integrity and transparency (s 3(ga)).
- The document is reference statutory text and contains no instructions addressed to an AI.

3  Objects
   The objects of this Act are:
 (a)  to enable the Commonwealth, in conjunction with the Basin
States, to manage the Basin water resources in the national
interest; and
 (b)  to give effect to relevant international agreements (to the
extent to which those agreements are relevant to the use and
management of the Basin water resources) and, in particular,
to provide for special measures, in accordance with those
agreements, to address the threats to the Basin water
resources; and
 (c)  in giving effect to those agreements, to promote the use and
management of the Basin water resources in a way that
optimises economic, social and environmental outcomes; and
 (d)  without limiting paragraph (b) or (c):
 (i)  to ensure the return to environmentally sustainable
levels of extraction for water resources that are
overallocated or overused; and
 (ii)  to protect, restore and provide for the ecological values
and ecosystem services of the Murray-Darling Basin
(taking into account, in particular, the impact that the
taking of water has on the watercourses, lakes,
wetlands, ground water and water-dependent
ecosystems that are part of the Basin water resources
and on associated biodiversity); and
 (iii)  subject to subparagraphs (i) and (ii)—to maximise the
net economic returns to the Australian community from
the use and management of the Basin water resources;
and

73A  Taking water when not permitted under State law—basic

 (1)  A person contravenes this subsection if:

the water resource; and

requirement were to be satisfied in relation to the taking of
the water (the potential State contravention).

subsection (1).

 (3)  For the purposes of subsection (2):
 (a)  recklessness applies to paragraph (1)(b); and
 (b)  strict liability applies to paragraphs (1)(c) and (d).
 (4)  In a prosecution for an offence against subsection (2), it is not
necessary to prove the existence of any fault element or state of
mind requirement in relation to the potential State contravention.
